# Periodisation Theory and Practice

## What Is Periodisation

Periodisation is the systematic planning of training variables — volume, intensity, exercise selection, and frequency — across time to produce peak performance, prevent accommodation, and manage cumulative fatigue. The term derives from "period" — training is organised into sequential time blocks (periods) rather than maintaining constant training parameters indefinitely.

Without periodisation, training eventually produces diminishing returns because the body accommodates to a fixed stimulus. Periodisation prevents accommodation by systematically varying the training stimulus and strategically managing the relationship between training stress and recovery.

## Macrocycle, Mesocycle, and Microcycle

Periodisation operates at three time scales:

**Macrocycle**: The entire training year (or multi-year plan for elite athletes). For recreational trainees, the macrocycle might be a 12-month plan with distinct goals per quarter (e.g., Q1: strength foundation, Q2: muscle building, Q3: fat loss and conditioning, Q4: performance peak).

**Mesocycle**: A training phase within the macrocycle, typically 3–6 weeks. Each mesocycle has a specific emphasis (accumulation, intensification, realisation). The mesocycle is the primary planning unit for most recreational trainees and coaches.

**Microcycle**: A single training week. The specific sessions, exercises, volumes, and intensities within the week. Microcycle design implements the mesocycle's goals on a day-by-day basis.

## Linear Periodisation

Linear periodisation is the simplest periodisation model and the most appropriate for beginner and early intermediate trainees. Across the macrocycle or mesocycle, volume decreases while intensity (load) increases:

**Phase 1 (weeks 1–4)**: High volume, low intensity. Example: 4×15–20 @ 60–65% 1RM. Builds work capacity and connective tissue tolerance.

**Phase 2 (weeks 5–8)**: Moderate volume, moderate intensity. Example: 4×8–12 @ 70–75% 1RM. Primary hypertrophy stimulus.

**Phase 3 (weeks 9–12)**: Low volume, high intensity. Example: 4×4–6 @ 80–85% 1RM. Strength development.

**Phase 4 (weeks 13–14)**: Very low volume, very high intensity. Example: 3×1–3 @ 88–95% 1RM. Peak strength readiness (appropriate only if a performance peak is desired).

Linear periodisation is less effective for intermediate and advanced trainees because it only develops one quality per phase. Its simplicity makes it excellent for teaching periodisation principles to novice trainees.

## Undulating Periodisation

Undulating periodisation varies training variables more frequently — either daily (daily undulating periodisation, DUP) or weekly (weekly undulating periodisation, WUP). This allows multiple qualities (strength, hypertrophy, endurance) to be trained simultaneously rather than sequentially.

**Daily Undulating Periodisation (DUP) example for a 3-day training week:**
- Monday: Power/strength focus — 4×3–5 @ 85–90% 1RM
- Wednesday: Hypertrophy focus — 4×8–12 @ 70–75% 1RM
- Friday: Endurance/metabolic focus — 3×15–20 @ 60–65% 1RM

Research (Rhea et al., 2002; Miranda et al., 2011) demonstrates that DUP produces superior strength and hypertrophy gains compared to linear periodisation over 12 weeks in intermediate trainees. The frequent variation in rep ranges and loads prevents accommodation more effectively than sequential phase training.

**Weekly Undulating Periodisation (WUP)**: Rep ranges change week-to-week rather than day-to-day. Example: Week 1 (hypertrophy, 8–12 reps) → Week 2 (strength, 4–6 reps) → Week 3 (power, 2–4 reps) → Week 4 (deload) → repeat.

## Block Periodisation

Block periodisation organises training into sequential specialised blocks, each developing specific qualities. Popularised by Vladimir Issurin (2008), it is the most sophisticated periodisation model and is primarily used by intermediate-to-advanced trainees.

**Three-block structure:**

*Accumulation block (3–6 weeks)*:
- Goal: Develop general work capacity and hypertrophy
- Volume: Very high (20–30 sets/muscle group/week for advanced trainees)
- Intensity: Moderate (60–75% 1RM, 8–15 reps)
- Focus exercises: High variety, multiple exercise angles, machines and free weights
- Residual effect: Improved work capacity lasts 25–35 days after block ends

*Transmutation/Intensification block (3–4 weeks)*:
- Goal: Convert hypertrophy and work capacity into strength and power
- Volume: Moderate (10–15 sets/muscle group/week)
- Intensity: High (75–90% 1RM, 3–8 reps)
- Focus exercises: Specific compound movements (squat, bench, deadlift, overhead press)
- Residual effect: Strength gains last 20–30 days after block ends

*Realisation/Peak block (1–2 weeks)*:
- Goal: Express accumulated strength; competition readiness
- Volume: Very low (4–8 sets/muscle group/week)
- Intensity: Very high (85–100% 1RM, 1–5 reps)
- Focus exercises: Competition-specific movements only
- Residual effect: Short duration; performance peak lasts 5–10 days

*Deload/transition (5–7 days)*: Reduce volume and intensity 40–60% to dissipate fatigue accumulated during the previous blocks before beginning the next macrocycle.

## Conjugate Periodisation

Conjugate periodisation, popularised by Louie Simmons and the Westside Barbell Club, develops multiple physical qualities simultaneously within each week rather than sequentially. It is primarily used for powerlifting-specific training.

**Westside Barbell structure (4 days/week):**
- Day 1: Max Effort Lower (work up to a 1–3 rep max on a squat or deadlift variation)
- Day 2: Max Effort Upper (work up to a 1–3 rep max on a bench press variation)
- Day 3: Dynamic Effort Lower (8–12 sets of 2 reps at 55–65% 1RM + accommodating resistance, performed explosively)
- Day 4: Dynamic Effort Upper (9×3 reps at 50–60% 1RM, explosive)

This system simultaneously develops maximal strength (ME days), rate of force development (DE days), and work capacity (accessory volume). It is highly specialised and not appropriate for general fitness training — the principles are most relevant for advanced strength athletes.

## Mesocycle Planning for Recreational Trainees

Most recreational trainees benefit from simple 4–6 week mesocycles with a deload week, repeating 3–4 times per year:

**Recommended mesocycle for intermediate trainees:**
```
Weeks 1–2: Moderate volume and intensity (ramp-up)
Weeks 3–4: High volume and intensity (peak stress)
Week 5: High intensity, low volume (intensity peak)
Week 6: Deload — 50% volume reduction, same exercises
```

At the end of each mesocycle, change 1–2 primary exercises, adjust rep ranges, or modify training frequency before beginning the next block.

## Periodisation for Different Fitness Goals

**Hypertrophy-focused periodisation**: Emphasise accumulation blocks with very high volume; shorter intensification blocks to prevent accommodation and maintain joint health. Volume is the primary driver — prioritise progressive volume increases across mesocycles.

**Strength-focused periodisation**: Emphasise intensification and realisation blocks; use accumulation for base building. Intensity is the primary driver. Percentage-based loading or autoregulation with RPE targets.

**Endurance-focused periodisation**: Macrocycle divided into base phase (high volume, low intensity, aerobic base development), build phase (threshold and tempo work), peak phase (race-specific intensity), and taper (volume reduction before competition).

**General fitness periodisation**: 2–3 month training blocks with alternating emphases (e.g., muscle-building block → fat loss/conditioning block → strength block). This prevents accommodation while aligning with seasonal motivation (e.g., conditioning block before summer).
