# Program Design for All Fitness Levels

## Beginner Program Design Principles

Beginners (0–12 months of consistent training) represent the highest potential for rapid adaptation and the highest risk of discouragement from programs that are too complex or too demanding. Effective beginner programs share consistent characteristics:

**Full-body training**: Beginners benefit most from training all major muscle groups 2–3 times per week rather than body-part splits. More frequent exposure to each movement pattern accelerates neuromuscular learning and produces faster initial strength gains.

**Linear progression**: Beginners can increase load every session (or every other session) because the rate of adaptation is so rapid. The Starting Strength model (Rippetoe) uses 3 sets of 5 reps and adds weight every session; this is appropriate for squats, presses, and deadlifts in true beginners.

**Low exercise variety**: 3–5 compound exercises per session is sufficient for beginners. The goal is to master the fundamental movement patterns (squat, hip hinge, horizontal push, horizontal pull, vertical push, vertical pull, carry) rather than accumulate exercises.

**Low volume**: 1–3 sets per exercise, 2–3 times per week per muscle group. Beginners do not need high volume — they respond to almost any training stimulus. Excessive volume leads to delayed recovery and discouragement.

**Simple structure**: 2–3 days per week, non-consecutive days (e.g., Monday/Wednesday/Friday). Rest days allow full recovery and accommodate real life.

**Beginner 3-day full-body template (equipment: barbell or dumbbells):**
```
Day A: Squat | Bench Press | Deadlift
Day B: Squat | Overhead Press | Barbell Row
Alternating A-B-A / B-A-B across weeks
```

**Beginner bodyweight template (no equipment):**
```
Day 1: Push-up (3×max), Squat (3×15), Plank (3×30s)
Day 2: Inverted Row (3×max), Reverse Lunge (3×10 each), Glute Bridge (3×15)
Day 3: Pike Push-up (3×max), Squat (3×15), Dead Bug (3×8 each)
```

## Intermediate Program Design Principles

Intermediate trainees (1–3 years) have exhausted the rapid gains of linear progression. Session-to-session strength increases are no longer achievable; weekly progress becomes the realistic target.

**Upper/lower splits**: Training upper and lower body on alternating days (4 days/week) allows higher per-session volume for each body region while maintaining adequate frequency. A standard template:
- Monday: Upper (push emphasis)
- Tuesday: Lower (quad emphasis)
- Thursday: Upper (pull emphasis)
- Friday: Lower (hip hinge emphasis)

**Push/pull/legs (PPL)**: Training push muscles (chest, shoulders, triceps), pull muscles (back, biceps), and legs on separate days. Can be run 3 days/week (one round) or 6 days/week (two rounds). The 6-day PPL provides each muscle group 2 sessions per week.

**Double progression**: Within a rep range (e.g., 3×8–12), increase reps each session until the top of the range is achieved across all sets, then add 2.5 kg and start back at the bottom of the range.

**Weekly periodisation**: Programming different rep ranges and intensities across the week. Example:
- Monday (strength focus): 4×4–6 @ 80–85% 1RM
- Thursday (hypertrophy focus): 3×8–12 @ 70–75% 1RM

**Intermediate 4-day upper/lower template:**
```
Day 1 (Upper Strength): Bench 4×4-6, Row 4×4-6, OHP 3×6-8, Pulldown 3×6-8
Day 2 (Lower Strength): Squat 4×4-6, RDL 4×6-8, Leg Press 3×8-10
Day 3 (Upper Hypertrophy): Incline DB Press 3×8-12, Cable Row 3×10-15, Lateral Raise 3×12-15, Curl 3×10-12
Day 4 (Lower Hypertrophy): Leg Press 3×10-15, Leg Curl 3×10-15, Lunge 3×12, Calf Raise 4×15
```

## Advanced Program Design Principles

Advanced trainees (3+ years) require block periodisation, higher specificity, and sophisticated management of fatigue accumulation.

**Block periodisation**: Sequential training phases (mesocycles), each with a specific emphasis:

*Accumulation block (4–6 weeks)*: High volume, moderate intensity. 4–6 sets per exercise, 10–15 rep range, 60–75% 1RM. Builds work capacity and hypertrophy.

*Intensification block (3–4 weeks)*: Reduced volume, high intensity. 3–5 sets per exercise, 3–8 rep range, 75–90% 1RM. Converts work capacity from accumulation into maximal strength.

*Realisation/peak block (1–2 weeks)*: Very low volume, very high intensity. 2–4 sets per exercise, 1–5 rep range, 85–95%+ 1RM. Peak performance readiness.

*Deload (1 week)*: 40–50% volume reduction before the next block begins.

**Autoregulation**: Using RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion, 1–10) or RIR (Reps in Reserve) targets rather than fixed percentage-based loads. This accounts for day-to-day variation in readiness and ensures appropriate training intensity regardless of acute fatigue state. An RPE 8 target means "stop the set when 2 reps remain in reserve."

**Exercise variation across mesocycles**: Rotate primary exercise selection between blocks to prevent accommodation and reduce repetitive stress. Example: primary squat variation rotates between back squat (accumulation), front squat (intensification), and safety bar squat (next accumulation).

## Program Selection for Specific Goals

**Goal: Build Muscle (Hypertrophy)**:
- Rep range: 6–20 (with emphasis on 8–15)
- Sets per muscle group: 12–20 per week
- Frequency: 2× per week per muscle group
- Rest periods: 90–180 seconds
- Exercise order: Compound movements first, isolation last
- Program examples: PHUL (Power/Hypertrophy Upper/Lower), Arnold split, PHAT

**Goal: Lose Weight (Fat Loss)**:
- Rep range: 8–15 (maintains muscle while allowing metabolic stress)
- Sets per session: Moderate volume; circuit training acceptable
- Frequency: 3–4 days resistance training + 2–3 days cardio
- Rest periods: 60–90 seconds (higher caloric burn per session)
- Prioritise: Maintaining muscle mass through adequate protein and resistance training
- Cardio: Added as caloric expenditure tool, not the primary training modality

**Goal: Improve Endurance**:
- Primary programming: Cardiorespiratory endurance sessions per FITT guidelines
- Resistance training: 2 days/week, full-body, moderate volume to maintain muscle
- Session structure: Resistance training before endurance in same-day sessions
- Recovery: Allow 48 hours between high-intensity endurance sessions

**Goal: Increase Flexibility**:
- Daily dedicated stretching sessions (20–30 minutes)
- Yoga or mobility-focused programming as primary modality
- Resistance training with full ROM on all exercises
- PNF stretching for targeted areas of restriction
- Patience: Structural flexibility changes require 8–12+ weeks

**Goal: General Fitness**:
- 3 days resistance training (full-body or upper/lower)
- 2 days moderate-intensity cardio (30–45 minutes)
- Progressive overload on both components
- Flexible programming that accommodates life while maintaining consistency

## Rest Day Programming

Rest days are not inactive — they are the time when adaptation occurs. Optimal rest day activities:

- Active recovery (walking, light swimming, easy cycling) to promote blood flow without adding training stress
- Dedicated mobility and stretching sessions (particularly beneficial for those with tight areas affecting training form)
- Sleep optimisation (targeting 7–9 hours; rest days are opportunities to extend sleep)
- Nutrition maintenance (continue meeting protein targets even on rest days; muscle protein synthesis is elevated for 24–48 hours post-training)

Avoid complete sedentary rest on every off day — NEAT maintenance and active recovery consistently produce better long-term results than alternating intense training with complete inactivity.
